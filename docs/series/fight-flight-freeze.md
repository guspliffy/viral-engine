# "Fight, Flight or Freeze": series format + pilot "You just robbed a bank" (10 Nov 2026)

**Pilot only.** Later episodes are written after Jonny has judged the finished pilot. The director thread writes the storyboard; builders only build. If Jonny sends panels, they win over this brief.

## What the series is

One extreme adult situation. Three friends. Each one picks a different instinct: fight, flight or freeze. We show all three outcomes in one stand-alone video (no follow-up parts), and the viewer argues in the comments about which one they would be. Adult tone (18-34): real stakes, a dark edge, only a little humour from the twist. Potato cast only.

## What stays the same in every episode

1. **25-32 s**, 9:16, 1080 x 1920, whole frame sharp.
2. **The situation is spoken, written and shown in the first 3-5 s** (Jonny's rule). Second person, present tense, three short sentences: "You just robbed a bank. You're hiding. The police are here." Narrator = engine voice `narrator` (voice 1, Jonny's commentator pick), first word at 0.0. The same words as the caption, one sentence at a time. Frame 0 already shows the whole situation.
3. **The three options** by about 6.5 s: the narrator says "Fight. Flight. Or freeze." and each word lands on one potato's face with a one-word label in the HUD style, so the viewer knows who picked what. Then the narrator plants the big question in one line: **"Only one of them gets away."** That question stays open until the last payoff.
4. **Three outcomes, stair-stepped:** each one is bigger or tenser than the one before. The order changes per episode and **the option that works changes per episode**, so nobody can guess it. Every outcome is a head fake: it looks like it works for a second, then it snaps the other way (or the reverse for the winner). Each outcome ends with one dry narrator line that closes it and opens the next one in the same breath ("One down."), so there is never dead air after a reveal.
5. **A fake-out before the last payoff** (Story Ladder snap 2): the last option looks lost for a moment, then turns.
6. **End card** with one line per potato on what happened to them, then the closing question **"Which one are you?"** and a hard cut.
7. House rules: sound at 0.0, stakes visible by 1.5 s, no title card mid-video (title only in-app, caption and the narrator's first line), no silent freeze before a punchline (when a character freezes, the sound never stops: siren, heartbeat, music), anything fatal stays off screen, gibberish always subtitled, characters react to each other, eyebrows on every reaction, no reaction signs, every potato that isn't cast is still alive, MrBeast playbook (frame 0 = the title, one wow).
8. Police, guards and bystanders are always our own cast in caps or uniforms. Never real brands, logos or a real police force's markings.

## Pilot: "You just robbed a bank" (30.0 s)

**Cast:** Viking = FIGHT (big, loud). Phoenix = FLIGHT (too much energy). Reaper = FREEZE (the scared one). All three in plain black eye masks, each with a money bag. Police: Samurai (lead officer, torch), Norse god and Dragon (shield line), all in dark police caps. The helicopter is only a spotlight and a rotor sound.

**Set:** one night street: an alley that opens onto a small market street with a vegetable stall (a crate of potatoes, price tag "POTATOES 2.99"). Police cars are painted plates with flashing blue-red lights. One painted rooftop plate for the chase. One painted beach plate for the last shot.

**Build notes**
- Where render time goes: the rooftop chase (11.5-18.5). Everything else stays cheap: one street set from three angles, painted plates for the cars, rooftops and beach.
- New props: money bags, eye masks, police caps, shields, net, torch, police van (back doors open and shut), potato crate with price tag. Check the library before building any of them.
- The net and the van doors need a contact check; Reaper's tip-over into the crate is a rigid fall (physics), not an eased pose.
- Reaper's frozen pose is ONE pose reused from 4.5 s to the beach. That is the running joke, and it is free.
- Music: a tense heist pulse from 0.0 that drops to one low note under the freeze and snaps to a bright beach guitar at 26.0.
- Mix: narrator 1-2 dB over the action; siren and alarm beds far under the voice (measure the stems, as for the wind fix).

## Hook notes from Jonny's two saved Kallaway posts (read 10 Nov from his TikTok favourites)

Posts: ["Dopamine addiction loop", 11 Oct 2026](https://www.tiktok.com/@kallaway.marketing/video/7695419696006352158) and ["Story Ladder, five levels of storytelling", 23 Oct 2026](https://www.tiktok.com/@kallaway.marketing/video/7699869751488564511). Read from the auto-captions, so the wording below is a summary, not his script.

| What he teaches | Where it is in the pilot |
| --- | --- |
| **Stakes** = a character to root for + something at risk + urgency | "You" are the robber (second person); prison is at risk; the torch beam is one step away |
| **The big question**: give just enough context that one question forms | "Only one of them gets away." at 6.0, open until 26.0 |
| **The head fake**: make the viewer assume A, then snap to B. He calls this the real hook, and wants hooks in the body too, not only the intro | Fight looks like it works, then the second police line. Flight: "Lost them!", then the van. Freeze: the torch stops on his face, then the price tag |
| **The rehook**: the moment a loop pays off, open the next one; longer videos chain loops instead of stretching one | "One down." / "Two down." / "Which leaves the one who did nothing." Three short loops inside one big one is what lets this run 30 s |
| **Arcs**: tension that rises and releases in waves | Loud and fast (fight), big and rising (flight), quiet and tight (freeze), release on the beach |
| **Your own fingerprint** (his top level) | A potato hiding in a crate of potatoes, and Reaper's one frozen pose from 4.5 s to the beach |

For the director: if a beat has to go for time, cut picture, never one of the four narrator handover lines. They are the loops.

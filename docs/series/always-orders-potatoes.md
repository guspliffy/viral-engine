# "That one friend who always orders potatoes…": series format (6 Nov 2026)

Jonny, 6 Nov (the day the PotatoBrawl TikTok got its first follower): our potato characters eat potato food in restaurants: fries, mashed potatoes, baked potatoes, oven potatoes and so on. **Viking eats them with pure joy while the others are shocked.** He said "go for it" to a series of briefs like Too Much Energy. This folder holds seven.

The joke is the one only this cast can tell: a potato happily ordering potatoes. It stays **implied and cute**. Nobody on screen says the word for it; the comments will ("he's a cannibal 💀"), and that is the comment trigger.

It fits the "that one friend who…" niche: everyone has the friend who orders the same dish in every restaurant and is far too happy about it. Viking is that friend, and the dish happens to be his own kind.

**Not the same as the later "always hungry" series (Dragon).** Viking is not hungry and does not eat everything. He only ever orders potatoes, in every place, however fancy, and he loves them.

Every episode brief in this folder follows this page. When an episode brief and this page disagree, the episode brief wins. If Jonny makes storyboard panels for an episode, his panels win over both (run `qa/panels.mjs`).

**Jonny's eight-panel storyboards for all seven episodes (6 Nov 10:19) are in storyboards/<episode>/storyboard-eight-panels.png** (character references in storyboards/references/, START-HERE.html is the gallery, REVIEW-NOTES.md the generator's own checks). Each brief has a "Jonny's storyboard" section with the per-panel notes. What the panels settle for the whole series:

- **Two rows, staged in depth** (this replaces the one-row table staging): the front row sits at the table, Gunslinger left, Viking centre, the right-hand friend (Reaper, or the Wizard in 2 and 6) right; the back row stands taller, Phoenix high at one back corner filming and the Samurai at the other (or back centre, his face above Viking's hair). The camera is a medium wide from the front at a little above table height, the dishes along the bottom fifth of the frame. Every face is visible in every panel.
- **The first-bite shot is a straight-on push-in from the front** (panel 02 in every sheet), Viking centred, eyes shut, cheeks pink, the side friends at both edges. Same framing every episode. It replaces the front-left three-quarter in the text.
- **The blossom:** white with a yellow centre, on the tip of his sprout. The drawn petals are sometimes rounder; build the five pointed star petals of the spec.
- **The sprouts:** the drawings only hint at the perky → bent → flat → limp steps; animate the four steps from the text.
- Timing badges are guides; the text's time codes, prop counts and grips win. No food in any panel has a face, which is right.

## What stays the same in every episode

**1. The same friend: Viking always orders potatoes.** Spiky orange hair with his little leaf sprout, cream fur collar, red tunic, brown belts, gold buckle, black gloves, as on his approved sheet (references/cast-sheets-v1/viking-reference-v1.png).
- **No axe in any episode** (restaurants; same rule as friends try to), including shadows. He eats with a fork, a spoon or his fingers, like people do with that dish.
- He is never creepy. He never looks at a friend hungrily, never jokes about eating them, never pokes them with a fork. He is simply, innocently delighted, and he can't understand why nobody else orders the potatoes.
- Voice: the fixed Viking Kokoro voice from the friends try to episodes (warm, loud, a big laugh).

**2. The same friends, each a fixed type.**

| Friend | Type | What they always do |
|---|---|---|
| **Gunslinger** | The one who loses his appetite | Slowly pushes his own plate away with one finger, eyes on Viking's plate. Hat tipped down over his eyes at the worst moment. No pistols in any episode (his holster holds a wallet, as in never has money). |
| **Reaper** | The silent stare | Slowly lowers his fork, then stares without blinking, the longest still hold in the clip. Leans back a little more each step. Says nothing. |
| **Phoenix** | The one who films everything | Has his phone up from the first second, phone clearly visible with a bright screen glow on his face (Jonny's never looks up rule, 5 Nov), a shutter `click` on the best moments. His wing tips flicker; never fire on a prop. |
| **Samurai** (guest) | The waiter who doesn't care | Brings the dish in frame 0 with a deadpan face. He is a potato serving potatoes and is completely fine with it, which makes it funnier. The same deadpan Samurai who works every job in never has money and "split the bill". No sword. |

**Wizard** is the occasional fourth friend (episodes 2 and 6): he tries a spell to swap the dish for something else, and it backfires into *more* potatoes (canon: the Wizard always messes up). In his episodes one of the fixed three sits out (Phoenix in 2, Reaper in 6; Gunslinger is in every episode because the first-bite block frames him), so **never more than five faces on screen**.

**3. The same two signatures, so viewers learn to wait for them.**

**THE FIRST BITE + THE BLOSSOM (1.50–2.80, the series sound logo).** Exactly the same moves, rhythm, sound and framing every episode:
- Framing (Jonny's panels): a straight-on push-in on Viking from the front, chest up, at his eye level, the dish in the bottom of frame, Gunslinger's shocked face at the left edge and the right-hand friend at the right edge (never covering Viking). This is the one cut allowed before 2 s.
- 1.55 he tucks a white napkin into his fur collar like a bib (`cloth`). 1.75 he lifts the first bite (a fry, a spoonful, a forkful: the episode says which) and looks at it lovingly.
- 1.95 the bite (the dish's sound: `ai/crunch` for crispy dishes, a soft `ai/sip`-style "mm" for soft ones). 2.10 his eyes close, his cheeks glow pink, a long happy **"Mmmmm!"** (non-word sound `hmm`, subtitled "Mmmmm!").
- **2.40 THE BLOSSOM: his leaf sprout pops open one little potato flower** (white star-shaped petals with a yellow centre, the way real potato plants flower; copy a real potato blossom). Sound: `pop`, the series sound logo, **once per episode, never anywhere else**.
- 2.40–2.80 he sways once side to side, blissful, the blossom bobbing.
- **The blossom stays on his sprout for the rest of the episode** (continuity) and bobs whenever he moves.

**THE SHOCK METER (works on mute).** The opposite of the blossom: **the friends' own two-leaf sprouts droop** one step at each escalation. Perky at frame 0, bent at the first joy move, flat at the second, fully wilted and limp at the peak. Every potato in the cast has the sprout on its sheet (on Gunslinger's hat crown, on the tip of Reaper's hood, on Phoenix's crest, on the Wizard's hat band), so the meter is always visible. Viking's blossom gets bigger and happier while theirs wilt. The Samurai waiter's sprout never moves.

**"WANT SOME?" (8.80–10.00).** Back to the wide. Viking, mouth full and blissful, holds out one bite on his fork toward one friend: **"Want some?"** (gibberish, subtitled). The friend leans back very slowly, eyes huge, sprout fully wilted. The music lands its last note on the offer, then room tone. **Hard cut at exactly 10.00** on the friend's face. No outro. (The finale flips it; its brief says how.)

**4. The same story shape, at the same time codes.** All episodes are **exactly 10.0 s**; hit-stops add 0.1 s each, so time the other beats around them.

| Time | Beat | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00–1.50 | **THE DISH LANDS** | Frame 0: the Samurai sets the potato dish down in front of Viking, already happening, with full sound (the plate clack, steam rising), in a wide with every face. Viking's joyful yell at 0.05 names the dish ("FRIIIES!", subtitled). The friends each have a different dish (salad, soup, steak, pancakes) and their heads snap to his plate. By 1.5 s, muted, a viewer knows: a potato is about to eat potatoes. |
| 1.50–2.80 | **THE FIRST BITE + BLOSSOM** | The series signature above. |

After each one the friends react: Gunslinger's plate push, Reaper's fork going down, Phoenix's shutter click, overlapping gibberish. The shock meter drops one step each time. **5. The same posting pattern.**
- In-app title (Jonny adds it with TikTok's text tool): **"That one friend who always orders potatoes…"** plus the episode's plain situation ("…at the burger place"). Keep the top 20% of the frame (y 0–384) clear from 0 to 2 s. Nothing is burned in except the gibberish subtitles.
- Caption line 1 matches the title, then **"Tag the friend who orders the same thing everywhere"**, then the series question **"What should he order next?"** (viewers pitch dishes and places, which feeds later episodes). Most episodes swap the question for a stronger pick-a-side question; the brief says which.
- Exactly 3 hashtags: #funnyanimation #animation + one dish tag. Never #viral or #fyp.
- **Cover frame: always THE BLOSSOM at 2.5 s**: Viking's blissful closed eyes and pink cheeks, the flower just popped open, the dish in front, Gunslinger's shocked face at the edge. The profile grid then reads as one set: the same face and flower, a different dish.

## What changes every episode

The restaurant (one layered painted env each), the dish, the friends' own dishes, the two joy moves, the peak, which friend gets the "Want some?", and the music (a different style, key and tempo every time, per Jonny's music-variety rule).

## Running jokes (comment triggers)

- **The joke nobody says.** A potato ordering potatoes. Never named on screen; the comments name it.
- **The blossom.** Viewers learn to wait for the `pop` at 2.4 s.
- **The wilting sprouts.** The friends' horror, told with their own sprouts.
- **The deadpan Samurai** works every job in town (cross-series with never has money).
- **Phoenix films everything,** so "where does he post these?" can become a comment.
- The finale (episode 7) pays it all off: someone else's sprout blossoms.

## Keep it cute, never gross (rules for every episode)

- **The food never has a face**, eyes, blush, arms, a sprout, or a shape that looks like a character. It looks like real, appetising restaurant food: golden fries, fluffy mash, crispy roasties.
- Nobody bites, cuts or peels anything that resembles a character. No knives near the camera. Chewing with the mouth closed; no food shown in an open mouth.
- The friends' horror is wide eyes, wilting sprouts, plates pushed away, a hat tipped down, a slow lean back. **No vomiting, no green faces, no gagging, no fainting into the food.**
- No dark music or stings on the joke; the music stays warm and happy, as Viking feels it.

## Rules every episode follows (from project memory)

- **Hook:** frame 0 is already action with full sound, the dish visible, full faces in a wide, motion. No cut before 1.5 s except the first-bite medium close at 1.50. It makes sense muted by 1.5 s.
- **Every gibberish line gets a subtitle** (Jonny, 4 Nov), the 0.05 yell and the "Mmmmm!" included. From 0 to 2 s in the lower third (y about 1240) so it never touches the in-app title; after 2 s classic style, 62 px, between y 470 and 1360 wherever no face is (caption-overlap check). Lines take turns and each ends before the next beat.
- **Conflict:** the friends visibly react to Viking and to each other: plate pushes, stares, shutter clicks, side glances at each other ("are you seeing this?"), overlapping gibberish. Nobody is mean to Viking, and he never notices that anyone is shocked; he reads their stares as envy.
- **Staging (Jonny, 5 Nov):** Viking and his dish own the front of the frame. No friend, waiter, glass or bottle ever covers his face, his hands or the dish. The friends react from the sides of the table.
- **Table staging in 9:16** (Jonny's panels): two rows in depth, as in the storyboard section above. Start from the split the bill numbers (camera about [0, 1.85, 4.75], fov 54) and move the camera in until the frame matches the panels. Raise the back row (standing, or on a step) so no back face hides behind a front one (face-hidden needs about 0.05 of screen-x clearance). Where the Samurai stands behind Viking, his face must be fully above Viking's hair and blossom. Keep the Samurai's deadpan look within about ±0.3 and lid ≤ 0.4 (the split the bill waiter's glitchy eyes).
- **Physics everywhere (Jonny, 5 Nov 22:00):** anything that falls, rolls, is flipped or toppled is simulated (template `physics: true`, `c.drop/throw/knock`), aimed so it lands on its story spot. Keep faces clear (face-hidden check).
- **Hands and contact:** 3-finger + thumb gloves. Every hand on a fork, spoon, cone, plate, phone or glass touches it with no gaps and no passing through (contact and rig checks #88, #94 must pass before any full render). Hands resting on the table use `{ prop: "table", grip: [x, TH, z], palm: true }`.
- **Sharp frame:** no depth of field (FOCUS=false, or focusBand 0.7). Separate characters from the background with light and contrast.
- **Place:** a layered painted environment (set type `env`, PR #92) per restaurant, about 3 minutes to make. Prompt for potato scale ("low view at table height…"). Real 3D geometry wherever a character or prop touches something (table, plates, glasses, benches). Six of the seven places are interiors: try the env first; if the coverage or look fails, use a painted plate plus 3D room geometry (the bistro in "split the bill": `engine/page/props-bistro.js`, `assets/plates/cosy-bistro.png`). Stay inside the env's safe camera range (about 0.9 m sideways, 4 to 10° of turn): no reverse angles. Painterly, Ghibli-like mood, our own look, lived-in detail, steam from every hot dish.
- **Look:** castLook "sheet" (hatched), light 2 from the template. Preserve the approved designs in references/cast-sheets-v1/. Clean anime linework, cel shadows, crisp faces. Eyebrows never touch the lid line.
- **No real brands** anywhere: no logos, chain names, ketchup labels or menu brands. Menus show a little potato doodle only.
- **Sound:** music under the whole clip with `music.lowCut` around 150 Hz; check `qa/sound.mjs <sb> --phone`. Only whitelisted sounds or Jonny's words (splort, dö-döm, bonk). Sounds used across the series: `pop` (the blossom only), `ai/crunch`, `ai/sip`, `ai/drip`, `click` (Phoenix's shutter), `put-down-glass`, `sit`, `cloth`, `thud`. New sounds go on the whitelist before use (list at the bottom).
- **Workflow:** start from storyboards/templates/clip-template.mjs (physics, hold 2, light 2, sound guard on). Fable reviews the storyboard and the cut. Merge only on Jonny's "merge it". Every delivery includes the posting card (title + caption + tags).

## Build tip: make the two signatures once

Build the first bite + blossom (1.50–2.80) and "Want some?" (8.80–10.00) as shared blocks, for example storyboards/series/always-orders-potatoes/first-bite.mjs and want-some.mjs, taking Viking's position, the bite prop, the edge friend and the camera. Build the shock meter as one helper (`wilt(friend, step, at)`, steps 0 to 3) and the blossom as a small prop parented to Viking's sprout with a 0.25 s pop-open (petals scale from 0 with a little overshoot). The bite, the "Mmmmm!", the blossom and the sound are then frame-identical across the series, and the frame cache can reuse more.

## Suggested posting order

1. **Fries at the burger place:** the simplest, most universal dish. It teaches the blossom, the wilting sprouts and "Want some?".
2. **Sunday roast (London pub):** a strong UK debate question and a good London location-tag test.
3. **Baked potato at the steakhouse:** US, loaded toppings, the toppling tower.
4. **Rösti at the ski hut:** Europe, winter, the flip.
5. **Gratin at the fancy French restaurant:** fine dining contrast.
6. **Mashed potatoes at Thanksgiving:** date-bound, **post on Thanksgiving, Thu 26 Nov 2026** (1 pm Berlin = 7 am New York, so US viewers see it on the day), whatever position that is. The friends try to Christmas tree posts Sat 28 Nov; keep it two days apart. It needs its render by about 23 Nov.
7. **Hash browns at the hotel breakfast (finale):** last, after at least four episodes, so Reaper's secret blossom lands.

Post them on separate days so each gets its own test group, alternating with too much energy and never has money. Keep the title pattern, caption pattern and posting time the same, so the episode is the only variable.

# "That one friend who never has money…": series format (5 Nov 2026)

Jonny, 5 Nov: a niche of relatable "that one friend who…" series, each friend trait its own series across different places (about 40 videos in total). He started with **too much energy (Phoenix)** and **never has money (Gunslinger)**, and liked every scenario idea: **restaurant bill, coffee IOU list, birthday gift coupon, bar round, taxi split, Christmas market free samples, and the twist where he has cash for himself (a sneaker drop).** This folder holds those seven. The too much energy series has its own folder and is written separately.

**Jonny's eight-panel storyboards for all seven episodes (5 Nov 11:40) are in storyboards/<episode>/storyboard-eight-panels.png** (character references in storyboards/references/). Each brief has a "Jonny's storyboard" section with the per-panel notes. The panels set the place, the camera side and shot size, who stands where, the light and the detail level; the text still sets the mechanics (timing, prop counts, grips, contact).

Every episode brief in this folder follows this page. When an episode brief and this page disagree, the episode brief wins. If Jonny makes storyboard panels for an episode, his panels win over both (run `qa/panels.mjs`).

## What stays the same in every episode

**1. The same friend: Gunslinger never has money.** Cowboy hat, red bandana, belts, as on his approved sheet (references/cast-sheets-v1/). He is never mean: he is charming, hopeful and shameless, and he genuinely believes "next time's on me".
- **No pistols in any episode, including shadows.** His holster on the right hip holds his **wallet**: an old, flat, brown leather wallet. Drawing it is his quick-draw. (This also keeps every clip safe for both apps.)
- He wears the same outfit in every place, plus one weather extra where the place needs it (a scarf at the Christmas market).

**2. The same friends, each a fixed type.**

| Friend | Type | What they always do |
|---|---|---|
| **Reaper** | The one who always ends up paying | Silent. A slow sigh, then pays without looking (a sleek black wallet or card, one calm move). His left eye twitches at the catchphrase. |
| **Wizard** | The one who keeps count | Calls Gunslinger out every time ("You said that LAST time!"), counts on his fingers, rolls his eyes, snickers when Gunslinger gets caught. Staff in hand or leaning nearby. |
| **Samurai** | Works every job in town | The guest in every paid place: waiter, bartender, taxi driver, market vendor, shop clerk. Deadpan, unimpressed, no sword. The same Samurai as the waiter in "split the bill" and the vendor in "The $20 Potato", so "how many jobs does he have?" becomes a running comment joke. |

Guests only where the place needs one (Viking's birthday in episode 4). Never more than four faces on screen.

**3. The same two signatures, so viewers learn to wait for them.**

**The POCKET PAT (1.50–2.80, the series sound logo).** Exactly the same moves, rhythm, sound and framing every episode:
- Framing: a medium on Gunslinger from his front three-quarters, chest up with the whole hat, at his eye level. The friend who is waiting for the money stays visible at one edge of frame. This is the one cut allowed before 2 s.
- Six pats, each the whitelisted `pat` sound (a soft cloth slap): **1.55 hat crown, 1.70 left chest, 1.85 right chest, 2.00 left hip, 2.15 back pocket, 2.30 right hip**. Hopeful face, eyebrows up, lips pursed.
- 2.35 **the quick-draw:** his right hand draws the wallet from the holster and spins it once round his three fingers like a revolver (`kenney/cloth-belt`).
- 2.50 he flips it open (`grab`). 2.55 a tiny puff of dust, and **the moth** flutters out (`wings`, very low gain, 0.6 s).
- 2.55–2.80 the moth circles his face once and lands on his hat brim. He looks at the empty wallet, then up at his friend with an innocent, toothy grin and a small shrug.
- The moth then stays on his hat brim for the rest of the episode (continuity), so it's still there at the wink.

**The WINK (8.80–10.00).** Gunslinger tips his hat with two fingers, winks and says **"Next time's on me!"** (gibberish, subtitled). The moth lifts as the hat tips, flutters once and settles back on the brim. One friend reacts: Reaper's left eye twitches, or Wizard facepalms. The music lands its last note on the wink, then room tone. **Hard cut at exactly 10.00.** No outro. (Episode 4 says "Next year's on me!", and episode 7 flips the meaning; the briefs say how.)

**4. The same story shape, at the same time codes.** All episodes are **exactly 10.0 s**; hit-stops add 0.1 s each, so time the other beats around them.

| Time | Beat | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0.00–1.50 | **PAY UP** | Frame 0 is the moment money is needed, already happening, with full sound, in a wide with every face: the bill tray lands, the card reader beeps, the round is called. A gibberish line at 0.05 asks for the money (subtitled). By 1.0 every head has turned to Gunslinger. Muted, a viewer knows "he has to pay" by 1.5 s. |
| 1.50–2.80 | **THE POCKET PAT** | The series signature above. |
| 2.80–7.00 | **THE DODGE** | The episode's trick, in two escalating steps, each one a habit real broke friends have. After each step the friends react: a glare, an eye roll, Wizard counting on his fingers, overlapping gibberish, Reaper's sigh. The first hit-stop sits on the cheekiest moment of the dodge. |
| 7.00–8.80 | **THE BILL LANDS** | The resolution: Reaper pays, or Gunslinger gets caught by his own dodge. Second hit-stop on the moment it lands. Its cause was set up on screen earlier (world logic). |
| 8.80–10.00 | **THE WINK** | The series signature above. Hard cut at 10.00. |

**5. The same posting pattern.**
- In-app title (Jonny adds it with TikTok's text tool): **"That one friend who never has money…"** plus the episode's plain situation ("…at dinner"). Keep the top 20% of the frame (y 0–384) clear from 0 to 2 s. Nothing is burned in except the gibberish subtitles.
- Caption line 1 matches the title, then **"Tag the friend who always says next time's on me"**, then the series question **"Where should he dodge paying next?"** (viewers pitch episodes, our missing comment trigger). An episode may swap the question for a stronger pick-a-side question; the brief says so.
- Exactly 3 hashtags: #funnyanimation #animation + one situation tag. Never #viral or #fyp.
- **Cover frame: always the moth at 2.6 s**: the open empty wallet, the moth fluttering out, his hopeful grin. On the profile grid the series then reads as one set: the same wallet and moth, a different place behind him.

## What changes every episode

The place (one layered painted env each), the reason to pay, the dodge, how the bill lands, and the music (a different style, key and tempo every time, per Jonny's music-variety rule).

## Running jokes (comment triggers)

- **The moth** is the same little moth every episode. It becomes a character people look for ("the moth is the real star").
- **Samurai works every job.** Same deadpan face in every uniform.
- **"Next time's on me!"** Next time never comes, and the finale (episode 7) pays that off.
- **The IOU notes** from episode 2 come back as the bill in episode 7.

## Rules every episode follows (from project memory)

- **Hook:** frame 0 is already action with full sound, the money moment visible, full faces in a wide. No cut before 1.5 s except the pocket-pat medium at 1.50. It makes sense muted by 1.5 s.
- **Every gibberish line gets a subtitle** (Jonny, 4 Nov), the 0.05 line included: classic style, 62 px, between y 470 and 1360 (inside the safe zone, never at the very bottom where the panels draw them: TikTok's caption covers that) so it never touches the in-app title, the lines taking turns, each ending before the next beat. Place a subtitle where no face is (caption-overlap check).
- **Conflict:** characters visibly react to each other: glares, eye rolls, finger-counting, blame, overlapping gibberish. Nobody is cruel to Gunslinger, and he never notices anyone being annoyed.
- **Staging:** the subject of the moment owns the front of the frame. A bystander never covers the main action (Jonny, tent episode). The wallet, the moth and whatever he dodges with must read on a phone.
- **Sharp frame:** no depth of field (FOCUS=false, or focusBand 0.7). Separate characters from the background with light and contrast.
- **Place:** a layered painted environment (set type `env`, PR #92) per place, about 3 minutes to make. Prompt for potato scale ("low view at table height…"). Real 3D geometry wherever a character or prop touches something. Stay inside the env's safe camera range (about 0.9 m sideways, 4 to 10° of turn): no reverse angles. Painterly, Ghibli-like mood, our own look, lived-in detail. Four of the seven places are interiors: try the env first; if the coverage or look fails, use a painted plate plus 3D room geometry (the bistro in "split the bill" did this: `engine/page/props-bistro.js`, `assets/plates/cosy-bistro.png`).
- **Look:** castLook "sheet" (hatched). Preserve the approved designs in references/cast-sheets-v1/. Clean anime linework, cel shadows, crisp faces. Eyebrows never touch the lid line.
- **Hands and contact:** 3-finger + thumb gloves. Every hand on a wallet, cup, card, note or box touches it with no gaps and no passing through. Hugs stay close but never intersect; arms wrap round the side. The contact and rig checks (#88, #94) and the env coverage check must pass before any full render.
- **Hit-stops and faces:** two 3-frame hit-stops per episode. Strong faces with long holds (lab picks). Gunslinger's grin is the face people screenshot.
- **Money on screen:** generic notes and coins only (plain green-cream notes with a little potato emblem, no real currency). Card readers show a glowing number, no brand. No real logos, shop names or film titles anywhere.
- **Sound:** music under the whole clip with `music.lowCut` around 150 Hz; check `qa/sound.mjs <sb> --phone`. Only whitelisted sounds or Jonny's words (splort, dö-döm, bonk). Voices are gibberish (Kokoro; Reaper A, Wizard B; pick one fixed Kokoro voice for Gunslinger in episode 1, warm and a bit drawly, and keep it for the series).
- **Nobody gets hurt,** nothing is stolen, and nobody is drunk (drinks in the pub are fizzy lemonade).
- **Workflow:** start from storyboards/templates/clip-template.mjs. Fable reviews the storyboard and the cut. Merge only on Jonny's "merge it". Every delivery includes the posting card (title + caption + tags).

## Build tip: make the two signatures once

Build the pocket pat (1.50–2.80) and the wink (8.80–10.00) as shared blocks, for example storyboards/series/never-has-money/pocket-pat.mjs and wink.mjs, taking Gunslinger's position, the waiting friend and the camera. The pats, the quick-draw, the moth's flight and the sound are then frame-identical across the series, and the frame cache can reuse more. Each episode builds 0–1.5, 2.8–8.8 and its reactor for the wink. Episode 7 calls the pocket-pat block twice, with a `cash: true` variant the first time.

## Suggested posting order

1. **At dinner:** the simplest place, and it teaches the pocket pat, the moth and the catchphrase.
2. **Coffee run:** introduces the IOU notes (needed for the finale) and is pure muted slapstick.
3. **His round:** the strongest comeuppance and pick-a-side question ("did they go too far?").
4. **Your birthday:** the wholesome twist and the strongest pick-a-side ("is a coupon a real gift?").
5. **In a taxi:** outdoor, rainy, and a good episode for the approved New York location-tag test.
6. **Christmas market:** post in the first week of December (markets open the last week of November; the friends try to Christmas tree posts Sat 28 Nov, so keep at least two days between them). Suggested: Thu 3 Dec.
7. **The sneaker drop (finale):** last, after at least five episodes, so the cash reveal lands. It needs episode 2's IOU notes.

Post them on separate days so each gets its own test group, and alternate them with the too much energy series. Keep the title pattern, caption pattern and posting time the same, so the episode is the only variable.

## Measure

For each episode at 24 h, 48 h and 7 days: views, average watch time, finish rate, and comments, shares and saves per 1,000 views (baseline 0, 0.1 and 0.5). Also count comments that pitch a new place (does the series question work?), comments that tag a friend, and comments about the moth or the Samurai (do the running jokes work?).

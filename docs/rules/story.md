# Story and house style: what makes a video ours

These are the producer's standing creative rules (Jonny, Oct-Nov 2026), collected from his notes on finished videos.
They apply to every new video, on this repo and on any fork. The engine rules (`docs/rules/output.md`, sound and checks)
and the order of work (`docs/rules/workflow.md`) still apply; this file says what the video must BE. Where a brief or a
series format says otherwise, the brief wins and the departure is named in the delivery. Series-only rules are in
`docs/series/README.md`. A new standing rule from the producer is added here the same day (see "Keeping rules in the
repo" in workflow.md).

How the producer judges: he watches the finished video on a phone, like a viewer, frame by frame. He judges videos,
not scripts or plans. Audience numbers decide packaging, not his score (`docs/rules/posting.md`).

## 1. The story

- **Suggestions, not a formula** (15 Nov, from the MrBeast study, on trial): the rules below guard against failures
  we have seen; they are not a recipe for the story. MrBeast's warning: strict beat checklists make every video look
  the same after about ten. So each new episode changes at least one visible thing about its shape from the last one
  in its series (opening, structure, setting, music, who wins), and the delivery names what changed. A series is
  reviewed after about 12 episodes and paused before it goes stale, even when it still does well.
- **Never the same outcome twice in a series** (18 Nov, ON TRIAL, from YouTube's monetization policy): YouTube does
  not monetize channels where "characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome"
  (templated storylines); a series with a fixed cast is fine when each episode has its own storyline. So a series
  keeps its format, but each new episode changes the situation AND the way it ends (who wins, what the catch costs,
  how it resolves), and the outline names how its ending differs from the last two episodes.
- **Can you picture it?** (15 Nov, on trial): before any work, the idea is written as one title line a stranger can
  picture at once ("world's largest firework" works; "a stock-picking monkey" bombed and no title or thumbnail could
  save it). If the picture is unclear, change the idea, not the packaging.
- **One chain of cause and effect** (10 Nov): every beat happens BECAUSE of the beat before it, written as "X, so Y,
  so Z" from the first second to the last frame. A beat joined by "and then", a time jump or a card is rewritten or
  cut. One mistake or one want starts the video and the last frame answers it. For every outcome ask "what on screen
  causes this?" and show the cause in the same shot or place; a character may say the reason in one subtitled line
  when the picture alone could be missed. "Elsewise the viewer is just wondering why he watched the video."
- **Mystery and explainer outlines use "But" / "Therefore"** (15 Nov): each beat after the first starts with "But"
  (a fact that breaks the last claim) or "Therefore" (the step it causes). Any "and then" beat is rewritten.
- **Speak in clear images** (15 Nov): each beat is one obvious action a child could name (he can't pay for the coffee;
  a gun at the counter; he puts the bags on the floor himself). Vague pictures (empty pockets, a mask) do not read.
- **Realistic, not absurd, when the title promises reality** (11 Nov): "more extreme" means a more extreme SCENARIO,
  not sillier events. Real stakes and consequences (hunger, thirst, no toilet, heat, tempers), a bit of comedy from how
  the friends behave, no absurd physics or magic escalation, an ending that follows from the situation.
- **Teach something real when the title promises know-how** (Survive it, 12 Nov): 5-6 real steps from real advice,
  each acted on screen and named; every need the story raises (water, food, a way out) is answered on screen.
- **World logic** (31 Oct): every turn, transition or background change has its cause on screen first, and every
  character in the shot follows it. Nothing grows out of nothing (branches attach to trunks). Copy nature: before a
  new scenery element, write down how it is in nature (spacing, sizes, clumps, what is broken) and build uneven.
- **The power is used on what it is about** (a money copier copies bills, not wallets). **The catch is the story's own
  consequence, shown big**, never a fun fact in lettering.
- **An outcome the story turns on gets its own shot** (the bear fleeing), not a turn inside another shot. When a
  character is meant to die or lose, show it (thrown out of the picture); a deadpan face is unclear.
- **A real fight is a real exchange**: lunge, block, hit, fall, with rigged opponents, never a crowd prop sliding.
- **Relatable, not random**: each character mirrors a real human trait in the situation ("that's me / that's my
  friend"). Research how real people behave first, then give each character one habit through their signature.
- **Show facts happening, never a host listing numbers** (explainers, 13 Nov): the host does something in every shot
  and others react, open on the evidence, a change at least every 3 s, numbers count up or punch in on screen.
- **Plan the feeling of each beat** (15 Nov, on trial, from the Dr Plants study): the outline names the ONE feeling
  the viewer should have at each beat (sympathy, awe, stress, relief, sadness, joy...), with at least three changes
  per video, and each beat is staged for its feeling. His ant film: one queen alone (sympathy), builds an empire (awe),
  war (stress), they win (excitement), the ants bury their dead (sadness). A beat with no feeling is cut. Viewers come
  for what happens to the character, not for the spectacle: a big moment no one cares about does not count.
- **A fact goes in only when it pushes the story** (15 Nov, on trial): every real fact is the cause of the next beat
  ("ants build graveyards", so after the war they carry their dead there), never a pause to teach. "As soon as the
  viewer knows they're learning, they leave."
- **A new turn about every 45 s in long films** (15 Nov, on trial): in a film over ~2 minutes, each block or part
  opens on a new twist, fact or question that re-hooks the viewer (MrBeast: one storyline can't hold a long video
  alone). Put them at the block joins, and say in the outline what each one is.
- **60 s+ videos: a turn every 10-20 s** (18 Nov, ON TRIAL, from the story study; a rule only once finished videos
  prove it): the "X, so Y" chain above and the But / Therefore outline now apply to every series, not only mysteries.
  In a 60-90 s video the outline shows a "but" (it gets worse or flips) every 10-20 s and names the one number that
  keeps moving in the picture (heat, minutes, money, people left). Model: a 1,120-subscriber channel's 8 min "stuck in
  a sauna with your crush" got 373K views in 3 days with a new, worse problem every 20-40 s.
- **Plant early, pay back at the end** (18 Nov, ON TRIAL): write the last line first, then plant 2-3 small things in
  the first quarter that the ending pays back or makes the viewer re-read (a brand-new wristband, a nickname used too
  early, an insult that comes back word for word). The outline lists each plant next to its payback.
- **Second loop at about 70 %** (18 Nov, ON TRIAL): just before the first question is answered, open a new one (a
  secret "there's something I need to tell you", the catch, a choice) so it carries the viewer through the climax.
  The outline names it.
- **A laugh at the low point; one break in every ladder** (18 Nov, ON TRIAL): the worst moment gets a small joke that
  releases tension without solving anything (the phone dies on the friend's text "How's it going? ;)"). An escalation
  ladder (the same joke, bigger each time) gets one step that breaks the pattern, so it does not go numb.
- **A narrator with a predictable opinion** (18 Nov, ON TRIAL): each series' narrator (or Spud) has one fixed attitude
  the viewer learns to predict; the facts come out of that trait ("when you get nervous, facts fall out of your
  mouth"). Narrator lines vary in length (short, medium, long), never all the same.
- **Science staircase** (any story around a real question, 14 Nov): stake and clock on screen, the real method tried
  at rising scale with each number SHOWN as a picture (4 friends fail, 40 creep, 250 make it), it nearly works, fails
  at the last step, then the turn. Real numbers carry a source on screen, own estimates are marked "≈".

## 2. The opening (first 3 seconds)

- **Build the video around its hook** (18 Nov, ON TRIAL on four videos; a rule only once finished videos prove it):
  the hook, the curiosity gap and the ending matter most, and the story is built between them. Write in this order:
  (1) a statement on screen in the first 3 s and the caption question about the same thing, both opening ONE gap
  ("He found the perfect crime." / caption "Did he really find the perfect crime?"), with the video already in the
  action; (2) the ending that releases that gap clearly (Court Day: three characters lined up, the last one reveals);
  (3) only then the beats between, each keeping the gap open. Court Day: 51 % finish, 58 % at 0:05.
- **Stake first** (12 Nov): the narrator's first line names WHAT is at risk and BY WHEN inside ~2.5 s ("One brother
  fries in ten seconds." / "You wake up mid plane crash. Ninety seconds to live."). Stake by relation ("he", "you",
  best friend, family), never a name, never the payoff, always true to the story. On screen: 2-4 words with the same
  stake and clock, upper third. Write the rest of the video to fit the opening; they are one chain. Voice 1 reads
  ~0.45 s a word: a 9-word line needs ~4 s, else keep it to 5-6 words.
- **The five opening rules** (11 Nov): (1) frame 0 is already the trouble, mid-motion, no walk-in or establishing shot;
  (2) two characters, one causing and one suffering, medium-wide, never one face or a small prop; (3) title, first
  subtitle and first frame say the same thing; (4) a voice at 0.0; (5) an open outcome by second 1 (a race, a
  countdown, a bet).
- **Opener type A or B**, named in the outline and the delivery: A = the contest is already running at frame 0;
  B = cold open, the ~1.5 s most extreme moment, then a clean cut back to the start.
- **An impossible fact or number opens best** (data, 15 Nov: "One number here should not exist." held 64 % at 0:03).
  Most viewers read German, not English: the first 3 s must read with no English (a number, an icon, a face).
- **Contrast in the hook** (18 Nov, ON TRIAL): the opening sets what the viewer expects (A) against what happens here
  (B), said or only shown ("Everything got 10,000x cheaper, except your money"; a sauna door that will not open at
  88 °C). A picture contrast reads with no English. Check the hook alone: can it be read two ways? Then rewrite.
- **"Remember that number"** (18 Nov, ON TRIAL): one number in the first 5 s that the narrator tells the viewer to keep
  ("$4,200. It's everything you have. Remember that number."), and that changes meaning by the end.
- **A hook caption from frame 0** (29 Oct), at most ~6 words, raising a question without the twist.
- **A video cut into short parts**: EVERY part opens on its own hook shot the long film does not have (14 Nov); a part
  that just continues the film loses viewers at the start.

## 3. Narrator, voices and text

- **The narrator leads** (15 Nov): in narrated series a narrator line on nearly every scene; "without it the story
  feels pointless". Voices: `engine/voice/voices.json`; voice 1 "deep storyteller" (`narrator`) for commentator and
  voiceover, voice 5 "trailer narrator" (`narrator-trailer`) for others.
- **Never two voices at once** (12 Nov): a character speaks only after the narrator's line ends (+ a breath). Time the
  cut list from the MEASURED speech lengths (`node qa/lines.mjs <sb>`), then place picture on it. Length follows the
  lines; never squeeze lines to fit. Queue narrate items BEFORE `c.beats()` (after it they are dropped) and check the
  narrator is in the render.
- **Lettering in the upper third** (12 Nov): spoken and narrated captions sit in the top third (above y 640, below the
  app bar at ~y 200), never over eyes or faces. Set `captionBand` to the upper third and take each caption's `pos` from
  `node qa/camfit.mjs <sb> --at <s>`. A face in the top third moves the camera or the caption, not the rule.
- **No call to action in the video** (18 Nov, replaces the 15 Nov follow-ask test): no spoken or on-screen ask to
  follow, share, comment, like or tag a friend ("Follow, or you'll miss part 2.", "Send this to your friend who...").
  The story ends on its payoff. Asks in the post itself (caption question, poll) are posting rules
  (`docs/rules/posting.md`), not part of this line.
- **Gibberish is always captioned** with its words, in the speaker's colour, timed to the line.
- **No title card or freeze mid-video** (9 Nov): the title is added in the app. No silent freeze before a punchline
  (it looked like a playback bug and lost a third of viewers in one second). Hit-stops and slow-motion close-ups are
  fine; a 10x moment plays straight through or as a speed ramp.
- **No reaction symbols** (anger veins, sweat drops, shock lines): faces and acting carry feelings.

## 4. Music and sound (details in `docs/rules/output.md`)

- **Music that supports the video on every video** (11 Nov): from 0.0 with the first picture, matching the mood,
  changing at the turn (the catch, the twist), under the voice (gain ~0.45 with a narrator; dramatic orchestra up to
  ~0.8 with 6 dB ducking). Each series has its own theme.
- **Dramatic scenario = dramatic recorded-sounding orchestra** (piano, trumpets, timpani), researched for the scenario,
  never a synth bed from `puppet/beat.mjs`; also in the FIRST draft (a stand-in bed is judged as the music). The music
  library's approved orchestra and disaster beds are the default for danger and chase scenes.
- **Realistic sounds only** (12 Nov): no blings, chimes, squeaks, boings or whooshes that only mark movement. A blast
  gets an explosion; zombies sound like zombies (`ai/zombie-groan`, `ai/zombie-horde`); a magic moment stays silent or
  uses a real sound (a heartbeat).
- **Mute test** (15 Nov, on trial): before a draft goes out, watch it once with the voice turned off (music and
  effects only). Every beat must still read and hold on picture and sound alone; a beat that dies gets a real sound,
  a music change or a clearer picture. Say in the delivery which beats failed and what changed.
- **Wind and room sound far under the mix** (`sound.ambience` ~0.06 with a voiceover, 0.1 without).
- The producer judges sound by ear: name the timestamps `qa/risky.mjs` picks.

## 5. Picture and cast

- **Clean, never glitchy** is the bar. Preflight-clean is not glitch-free: before a draft goes out, look at EVERY body
  and held prop in EVERY shot on a 1 fps sheet and a close still per contact (`docs/rules/clip-lessons.md`).
- **Follow the producer's panels exactly**: one shot per panel, camera side, who is in front, eye lines, time of day,
  detail level. "I create these images with purpose, not to be ignored." Write PANELS.md first; a wanted departure is
  asked BEFORE the build.
- **Detail and beauty**: every shot is a picture worth looking at on its own, wide shots never bare, a real set or
  plate behind every shot (never a blank wall). The whole frame is sharp (no depth of field).
- **Physics on everything that falls, is thrown, knocked or piled** (template default), never tweened.
- **Nobody floats**: a hanging character has body and hands ON the thing; feet are on something.
- **New characters, costumes and creatures**: the hand-drawn Ghibli look, very detailed, every mark drawn once (no
  repeating textures), sleeves end before the wrist. Animals look real (real proportions, small real eyes, one
  continuous skin), never spheres stuck together with toy eyes.
- **Cast colours stay**: never bake, char or darken the cast's skin for a whole clip (soot marks at most).
- **Side characters are designed for the scene** (no fighter cast as a random rich couple).
- **Pace**: no shot over ~1.8 s in the first half unless something moves in it; energy high-low-high. Hold the last
  shot ~1.5-2 s after the last line so the ending breathes. Length follows quality: "there is no reason to rush".
  No slow motion under the spoken opening.
- **Food series**: every potato that gets eaten is ALIVE (face, reaction, ideally a line).
- **Held cards and phones**: cards face the holder (show a hand with an over-the-shoulder shot); a phone shown to
  others uses the edge hold (`docs/rules/clip-lessons.md`).

## 6. The cast (who is who: `docs/character-bible.md`, reference sheets in `references/cast/`)

- **Spud** is the lead of everyday videos: one neutral potato (teal knit sweater, one leaf) the average person
  identifies with; he goes along with his friends.
- **Everyday friends**: Chip (yellow hoodie, too much energy), Wedge (red bomber, "trust me"), Mash (charcoal hoodie,
  never looks up), all with Spud's skin colour.
- **The ten fighters** (Astronaut, Viking, Reaper, Gunslinger, Dragon, Norse god, Samurai, Wizard, Sorcerer, Phoenix)
  belong in contests where the viewer picks a side (powers, duels, game shows, votes), which is also the marketing
  for the PotatoBrawl game. Canon signatures: the Wizard messes up, the Samurai slices, the Viking destroys, the
  Gunslinger shoots.
- Not on this channel: humans, Pebble, Mott (a separate calm account).

- Set “One of them is too heavy.” in two bold lines against clear sky in the upper third.
- Frame all four in a medium-wide three-quarter shot, with the tall friend left of center and the jetty at frame right.
- Freeze all four mid-motion and aim their eyes at one thin white crack running between their boots.
- Keep the hole, both rods and bucket visible below the group, and let one reel handle peek from the tiny friend's lumpy hoodie.

## Panel 2

- Favor the tiny and biggest friends in a medium shot and have the tiny friend point straight up at the biggest friend's face.
- Turn the biggest friend toward the finger with raised palms and a scowl, and aim the other two friends' eyes at him.
- Keep all four on the ice and extend the same crack slightly in the clear space between their boots.
- Place “You're too fat.” in open space above the pointing gesture.

## Panel 3

- Angle across the right-hand jetty and catch the biggest friend planting one boot on the raised planks, arms out and face turned back in a scowl.
- Keep the other three visibly on the ice and branch the crack behind the departing boot toward them.
- Put “Fine!” in the upper third over the biggest friend's side of the shot.

## Panel 4

- Hold the jetty angle and show the tall friend in profile, stepping up beside the biggest friend with his eyes fixed on his phone.
- Group the tiny and teal friends by the fishing hole and extend the crack branches toward their feet.

## Panel 5

- Bring the tiny and teal friends into the foreground in a medium shot and aim the tiny friend's index finger up at the teal friend's chest.
- Keep the tiny friend half the teal friend's height and give the teal friend hunched shoulders, wide eyes and a visible bead of sweat.
- Show the other two on the jetty at right, with fresh crack branches at the two friends' feet on the ice.
- Put “Your turn.” in the upper third above the pair.

## Panel 6

- Widen to show the teal friend tiptoeing onto the right-hand jetty, with his lead boot on the planks and his trailing boot lifting clear of the ice.
- Stage the other two on the planks and leave the tiny friend alone in the middle beside the hole, two rods and bucket.
- Run fresh white branches toward the tiny friend's boots across the open ice.

## Panel 7

- Drop to ice level and frame the tiny friend's whole body, with eyes wide, mouth open and feet planted apart.
- Spread a broad spiderweb of bright cracks around both boots and keep the fishing hole visible in a foreground corner.
- Keep the hoodie bulge and small reel handle visible beneath his centered face.

## Panel 8

- Shoot over the tiny friend's shoulder, keeping his sprout low in frame and all three faces above the right-hand jetty rail.
- Lean the biggest friend over the rail with a suspicious squint, have the tall friend glance up from his phone and turn the teal friend's eyes toward the hoodie.
- Place “Wait...” above the biggest friend in the upper third.

## Panel 9

- Cut to a low close shot that holds the tiny friend's guilty grin, hoodie hem and the ice where the reel is about to land.
- Show a recognizable spool and crank falling clear of the hem, with both hands away from the fabric.
- Leave the crack branches visible around his boots and clear the “Wait...” caption before the cut.

## Panel 10

- Frame the tiny friend and the landing area together as he lifts the hoodie and gear pours visibly from the opening.
- Stagger the spill from sinkers and reels to the tackle box and ice drill, then land the black iron anchor last in clear view.
- Hold the crack pattern from panel 9 through the anchor's impact.

## Panel 11

- Frame the tiny friend grinning with arms wide beside the pile and silhouette the anchor on top.
- Fit all three friends behind the jetty rail at right and give them wide eyes and open mouths.
- Place the complete “Just in case!” in clear space in the upper third.

## Panel 12

- Widen to a three-quarter view of the pile and jetty, and trace a fresh crack all the way around the slab before it tilts steeply.
- Show the raised edge and dark water below as the whole pile, anchor included, slides down the slope into one big splash.
- Keep the tiny friend smiling on solid ice beside the rods and bucket, with his empty hoodie hanging flat and the fishing hole separate from the splash.
- Hold long enough to read all three friends laughing together on the jetty.

## Five most important changes overall

1. Keep the jetty at frame right and show each step onto it, so the number on the ice drops visibly from four to three to two to one.
2. Build one readable crack pattern from a hairline to a spiderweb around the tiny friend, hold it during the spill, then isolate the gear slab with a fresh crack.
3. Show the cause and effect of the gear gag in order, from the hoodie bulge and falling reel to the anchor landing last and the whole pile sliding into the lake.
4. Keep the tiny friend half the teal friend's height and give each upward point a clear target, matching eye lines and a distinct reaction.
5. Keep faces and key gestures clear of the rightmost 15% and bottom 20%, and hold each complete caption in clear upper-third space with a dark outline.

1. Odin paid an eye for one sip of the well of wisdom, so Chip, who has a test tomorrow and studied zero, wants that sip.
2. The well's guardian wants one eye, and Odin's own eye still looks up from the bottom, so Chip looks for a free way.
3. Odin hung nine nights on the tree for the runes, so Chip tries one night, but he lasts four seconds.
4. Odin's two ravens see everything, so they snitch that Chip never studied, and the sneak sip gets caught.

## Facts (Prose Edda + Poetic Edda; standard Norse myth, no exotic claims)
- Mimir's well (Mimisbrunnr) lies under one root of the world tree Yggdrasil; its water holds wisdom; Odin got a drink
  by pledging one eye, and the eye lies in the well (Voluspa 28, Gylfaginning 15).
- Odin speaks with Mimir's head (Voluspa 46; Ynglinga saga: the head is kept and gives advice). We put the talking
  head IN the well as the guardian: story choice joining both sources.
- Havamal 138-139: Odin hung on the windy tree nine nights, no bread, no drink, and took up the runes.
- Odin's two ravens (Huginn = thought, Muninn = memory) fly over the world every day and tell him what they saw
  (Grimnismal 20, Gylfaginning 38). No names on screen.
- Wednesday = Old English Wodnesdaeg, "Woden's day"; Woden is the English name of Odin.
- Potato "eyes" are the real name of the buds on a potato tuber.

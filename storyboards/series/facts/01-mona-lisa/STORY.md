## Facts and sources (checked 19 Nov 2026)

- Vincenzo Peruggia worked for the glazing firm that put protective glass on Louvre paintings, the Mona Lisa among
  them; hid in a storeroom/closet overnight; Monday 21 Aug 1911 the museum was closed; took her off the wall, removed
  the frame and glass in a stairwell, wrapped her in his white workman's smock; a plumber let him through a locked door.
- The theft was noticed the next day, when a painter (Louis Béroud) came to copy her; guards assumed she was being
  photographed. The Louvre closed for a week. Picasso was questioned; Apollinaire was jailed briefly.
- When it reopened, crowds came to look at the empty wall (Kafka and Max Brod among them).
- Police interviewed Peruggia at his room and accepted his alibi; she was in a trunk there for about two years.
- Dec 1913 he offered her to a Florence dealer (Alfredo Geri), who called the police; many Italians saw him as a
  patriot; sentenced to a year and 15 days, cut to about seven months on appeal.
- CAVEAT: critics already rated the painting before 1911; it was the public that walked past her. Say "nobody stops
  for her", never "nobody knew her".

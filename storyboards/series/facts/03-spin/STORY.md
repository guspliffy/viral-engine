## Facts and sources
- PopSci "What would happen if the Earth started to spin faster?" (S. Fecht; Sten Odenwald NASA, Witold Fraczek ESRI):
  even +1 mph moves geostationary satellites off station (TV, comms); +100 mph equator lowlands 30-65 ft under;
  ~+1,000 mph (about double) "hundreds of feet" of water at the equator, only the highest peaks (Andes, Kilimanjaro) dry;
  hurricanes spin faster with more energy; ~17,641 mph (about 17x) weightless at the equator, "reverse rain";
  ~24,000 mph crust flattens at the poles, bulges at the equator, enormous earthquakes; Earth had ~4 h days right after
  the Moon-forming impact; "no conceivable way" Earth could spin up like this.
- My math: weight lost at the equator = 0.35 % x k^2 (10x 35 %, 12x half, 15.5x = Moon gravity 1/6, 17x all);
  day = 24 h / k. Not said as sourced. Skipped on purpose (sources skip it too): the jolt winds of a sudden speed-up.
- Ring: my guess for material flung off at orbital speed (shown, never said as fact). Flat disc = the direction a fast
  spin pushes (sourced flattening), exaggerated for the punchline.
- Quito: ~0.2 deg S, ~2,850 m, high in the Andes = on the equator and above the flood.

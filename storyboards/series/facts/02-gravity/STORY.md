## Facts and sources
- 2 g: you weigh double (physics).
- 3 g: the Space Shuttle's launch was held to about 3 g (PBS NOVA, "All About G Forces").
- 3-4 g vision greys, ~4.5-6 g untrained people sitting up black out (Wikipedia G-LOC; Air & Space Forces "G-Lock and the Fighter Jock").
- Pilots reach ~9 g only with a g-suit and straining (Air & Space Forces).
- Lying back (eyeballs-in) is tolerated far better: untrained subjects held ~4.5 Gx for 2 min (NASA centrifuge, ASMA 2021, Murphy);
  6 Gx for 2 min drops blood oxygen to ~86 % with severe breathlessness (Robbins et al. 2021, Aerosp Med Hum Perform 92(7)).
  Short Gx limits ~20 g for ~6 s (1964 NASA databook, secondary) -> "No human survives this" at 25 g.
- Buildings: design load factors 1.2 dead + 1.6 live (ASCE 7 LRFD) -> about 1.5-2x margin (estimate, said as "about twice").
- Air (Earth's mass x g, atmosphere kept): surface pressure x g; scale height 8.5 km / g, so at 20 g half the air is below ~300 m.
  Oxygen partial pressure 0.21 bar x g passes the divers' toxicity limit 1.4-1.6 bar (DAN, NOAA) from ~8 g.
- Orbits: a circular orbit's speed is too slow once the pull grows: new perigee r / (2g - 1). Geostationary falls from ~4 g,
  so by 30 g every satellite falls; the Moon's perigee drops inside Earth from ~31 g; at 50 g it hits in ~17 h (half orbit).
- Mountains: height is capped by rock strength at the base (rho g h): Olympus Mons 21.9 km = 2.5x Everest on Mars' 0.38 g
  (MOLA; EarthDate "How tall can Earth's mountains get"). Everest's base at 40 g: ~9 GPa vs granite ~0.2 GPa.
- No source: birds can't fly, the sofa breaks (pictures only, never said as facts).

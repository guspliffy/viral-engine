# What if your friend could pause YOU?

**Original Potato Brawl example · 45-second proposed master · 13 November 2026**

Status: evergreen creative hypothesis, not a predicted viral hit or a produced video. Written for this 30-second–5-minute research brief. Existing 19-second episodes remain unchanged.

## The story in one paragraph

Reaper reaches for the last cookie. Wizard freezes him with a magic remote and takes it. When Wizard releases him to show off, Reaper throws a cushion. Wizard freezes him again, but the cushion keeps flying and knocks the cookie back onto the table. Reaper has learned that the spell stops a person, not everything around them. Released again, he notices Wizard's reflection in the metal serving tray. He raises it as a shield. Wizard's next spell bounces back and freezes Wizard. Reaper wins, but chooses to split the cookie. He uses the same reflection to release Wizard, who now thinks twice about cheating.

## Why this is a useful test

- **Immediate intention:** reach the last cookie.
- **Immediate unfair obstacle:** the other friend can stop your body.
- **Forward question:** how can the apparently powerless friend win?
- **Early rewards:** an interrupted reach, Wizard's smug shortcut, then the cushion arriving despite the freeze.
- **Changing tactics:** reach → indirect attack → observed loophole → reflected power.
- **Fair reversal:** a reflective tray is part of the table arrangement from the opening.
- **Emotional finish:** the winner shares; the power imbalance is resolved without making either friend permanently cruel.
- **Share hypothesis:** “That friend who cheats even when the stakes are one cookie.”

This is a candidate for the user's 50–70% three-second retention goal. No source or script can establish that it will achieve the number before testing.

## Cast and production footprint

**Reaper:** recognizable golden potato body, green sprout, charcoal hood/cape, round silver clasp, tired expressive eyes. His performance is understated irritation becoming quiet ingenuity. No scythe.

**Wizard:** recognizable golden potato body and green sprout, floppy purple pointed hat and purple cape. Cleverness becomes overconfidence, then embarrassment. Keep the existing clean anime identity; do not substitute the separate humanoid redesign proposals.

**Set:** one bright shared-flat room, one low table and a sofa within arm's reach. Keep the camera on a consistent side of the table. The characters, cookie and remote should read at phone size. Keep decorations quiet.

**Props:** one large readable cookie, one small plate on a polished metal serving tray, one cushion, one clearly recognizable remote with a large pause-symbol button. The pause symbol supports the visual rule; no English text is necessary to follow the action.

**Visual style:** golden matte characters, clear silhouettes, controlled contours and cel shading. Use anime emphasis for the ridiculous snack duel: purposeful anticipation, a brief confident pose and a decisive reflected-spell beat. Preserve grips, eye lines and object paths. No obligatory camera movement between story changes.

## The power must remain consistent

The remote projects a visible magical ray. **A hit toggles a living target between frozen and moving.** It does not stop the whole room. A physical object already released continues moving. The ray can reflect from the polished tray; this is a rule of the fictional device, not a claim about real physics or time travel.

Frozen means the whole target holds its pose: no moving eyes, lips, breathing or unexplained escape. Emotion during a freeze comes from the held pose and the moving character's behavior. The thaw restores movement with a distinct cue.

The remote stays in Wizard's hand throughout. Reaper does **not** pull it out of frozen closed fingers. To release Wizard, Reaper presses its accessible button while holding the tray at the established reflection angle. This preserves the same mechanism and avoids an impossible grip transfer.

## First three seconds: the hook

These timings are production proposals, not research-derived requirements.

| Time | Picture | What the viewer should understand |
|---|---|---|
| **0.00–0.35** | Close enough to see two faces, Reaper's already-reaching hand, the last cookie and Wizard's thumb on the remote. The shiny serving tray is visible beneath the plate. | Two friends want one snack; something is about to interrupt the reach. |
| **0.35–1.20** | A clear click and short ray. Reaper stops with his fingers one inch from the cookie. Wizard remains moving. | Wizard has unfair control over Reaper. |
| **1.20–2.20** | Wizard's eyes move from the frozen hand to the cookie. He slides his own hand under it and picks it up. | He is using the power to take the snack. |
| **2.20–3.00** | Wizard raises the cookie just beyond the frozen hand, with a tiny self-satisfied smile. Keep remote and frozen reach visible. | A small injustice has happened; the contest is unresolved. |

The opening contains a consequence and a comic attitude. It does not require a logo, explanation of the device's origin or a spoken “what if.” The title can live in the caption/cover. If an on-screen title is used, it must not cover the face, hand, ray or cookie.

| Time | Action and acting | Reason to keep watching | Staging / sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0–3s** | The interrupted reach described above. Wizard takes the cookie. | How can Reaper beat the unfair power? | One readable two-shot. A single activation click; a short, consistent freeze tone. |
| **3–7s** | Wizard holds the cookie beside his face and admires his own cleverness. He points the remote back at Reaper and releases him, intending to demonstrate how easily he won. Reaper completes the reach into empty space, then looks from Wizard to the cushion beside him. | Reaper is forming a response. | Give the completed empty grab a short comic beat. Eye movement precedes reaching for the cushion. |
| **7–15s** | Reaper quickly throws the cushion. Wizard reacts with the remote and freezes Reaper in the follow-through, clearly **after release**. Wizard smiles too early. The cushion keeps moving and lands softly in his face; the cookie drops onto its plate. | The power has a limit, and the supposedly unbeatable character can be surprised. | Preserve one continuous cushion path. Small impact, clear cookie landing. This is the first substantial reversal, not a detached gag. |
| **15–20s** | Wizard removes the cushion with his free hand and releases Reaper again, now treating this as a contest. Reaper looks down at the tray and sees a clear reflection of Wizard and the remote. A restrained eye change shows the idea. | The viewer can anticipate a new tactic. | Show the reflection clearly enough to understand. No explanatory caption or added power. |
| **20–26s** | Reaper slides the cookie plate off the tray onto the table, takes the tray by its handle and approaches. Wizard aims. Reaper raises the tray just in time; the visible ray reflects back onto Wizard. Wizard freezes in his own triumphant pose. | The planted object pays off; status reverses. | Use a medium view that contains remote, tray and return path. A brief anticipation before the ray is more useful than several confusing cuts. |
| **26–32s** | Reaper lowers the tray enough to see Wizard's frozen face, takes the cookie and brings it toward his mouth. | **32–40s** | Reaper breaks the cookie into two pieces and places Wizard's half on the plate. Gentle cookie snap; distinct thaw sound. |
| **40–45s** | Wizard briefly raises the remote as if considering one more trick. Reaper casually tilts the tray so Wizard sees his own reflection. Wizard immediately lowers the remote. No mandatory closing question or forced loop. |

The timed draft should be adjusted if the animatic demonstrates that an action cannot be read or is being held too long. Forty-five seconds is a proposal for a complete story, not a reason to pad a natural 40-second cut or rush a needed reaction.

## English wording, if wanted

- **Title / cover:** “What if your friend could pause YOU?”
- **Optional caption:** “He brought magic to a cookie fight.”
- **Dialogue:** none required. Brief nonverbal reactions can add personality.

The essential story remains understandable with captions hidden and sound muted. Sound should make it more enjoyable, not repair an otherwise unreadable action.

## Main risks and how to catch them cheaply

| Risk | Animatic check / correction |
|---|---|
| The freeze resembles a playback error | Keep Wizard moving immediately beside the frozen hand; show the click and ray. |
| Viewers cannot identify the remote | Increase its silhouette and button size; reduce competing props. |
| The viewer thinks time itself stopped | Preserve the cushion's continuous motion and avoid freezing the environment. Title says “pause YOU.” |
| The tray solution feels invented at the climax | Show the tray from the beginning and a legible reflection at 15–20s. |
| A mirror bounce is unreadable on a phone | Stage a clear ray path in one view; simplify the camera angle before adding effects. |
| Wizard seems pointlessly cruel | Keep the rivalry petty, the cushion soft and the final acceptance warm. Limit smugness; do not prolong helplessness. |
| Reaper's victory is already complete at 26s, causing an exit | Move directly into the meaningful choice to share; keep the final release and tag concise. Inspect retention around this transition. |
| The cookie/remote changes hands invisibly | Keep Wizard's remote in the same hand; show the dropped cookie landing; keep the plate on the table when the tray is raised. |
| The final unfreeze violates the established rule | Use the same button, reflection path and target. Do not introduce an automatic timer. |

## Opening comparison

Use private animatics first. Both versions use the same framing, visible props, audio policy and total running time, and converge at 3 seconds.

- **A — consequence immediately:** the freeze activates almost immediately as the already-extended hand reaches the cookie.
- **B — brief anticipation:** the viewer sees the hand approach and Wizard prepare the remote for roughly one additional second before the same freeze.

Changed variable: **time/information order before the first power consequence**. Keep text and the remaining film fixed. The hypothesis is that A reduces waiting, while B may improve causal comprehension. Neither is declared the winner in advance.

Primary question: which opening improves the appropriate native early metric without worsening comprehension or continued viewing? The project target is 50–70% at literal second three where available. YouTube stayed-to-watch remains a separate metric.

A private, small-viewer comparison is a comprehension screen. Subsequent organic publication of different episodes is exploratory; duplicate public uploads would not supply a clean randomized test.

## Longer versions

**60–90 seconds:** extend only if a genuinely different tactic creates a new consequence. For example, the first indirect tactic could reveal a cost to Reaper, forcing him to choose the reflective solution. Do not add an extra failed reach merely to reach a minute.

**2–3 minutes:** the cookie duel alone is likely too thin. Develop a larger objective: the friends need to prepare a surprise together, and Wizard's freezing shortcuts progressively make cooperation impossible. The final shared action solves the goal that the power could not.

**3–5 minutes:** build a second movement with a changed objective and an earned relationship choice. Treat it as a new episode brief with a full script, rather than a slowed version of this one. The full episode would use YouTube's ordinary-video route; any short companion should resolve its own local problem.

## Production and measurement record

- Hypothesis: a visible unfair power, a recoverable contest, changing tactics and a planted reversal will produce a clearer reason to continue than an explanatory setup followed by disconnected demonstrations.
- Fixed choices: golden cast, clean anime direction, two characters, one set, English packaging, visually complete action, common 45-second master.
- Changed variable in the first focused comparison: opening information order only.
- Outcomes: native early retention/stayed-to-watch separately, watch seconds, retention near the cushion and reflection payoffs, completion, views, unique reach, shares/sends and follows.
- Comparison age: 24h, 48h and 7d, actual timestamps recorded; within-platform analysis.
- Publication status: no animation render, upload, scheduled post or paid distribution performed.
